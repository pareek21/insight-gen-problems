Every attempt's reasoning, cut into windows and labelled by GPT-6.1 Sol against the reference proof's 12 claims and a catalogue of approaches outside it, drawn as a walk over one shared map, one map per round of hints.

  • With the standard prompt and no hint, Kimi proves the standard bounds and stops. All four attempts (20–108K characters) are entirely off the map: 46–66% on the standard bounds (the product construction, Fekete's lemma, the factorial upper bound), 20–31% on the literature and up to 23% on Schur-type colourings. All four call the problem open.
  • r1's recursion sends both attempts looking for the cross-edge rule. 67–70% of each attempt is on the proof, nearly all of it on the two steps r1 leaves out: the coordinate cover (27–35%) and the cross-edge rule (15–26%). Both conclude that the hinted construction fails at edges between two blocks (Sol notes that their objection only covers colouring a pair of blocks uniformly) and call the problem open.
  • r2 gives the rule but not the maps f and g, and both attempts spend the hour on them. 99% of each attempt is on the proof and 83–87% on a single claim, constructing f and g (S3), until both are cut at the one-hour limit.
  • With the random matrix (r3), all four attempts prove the theorem, in a fifth to a quarter of the length. 93–99% is on the proof, in 103–147K characters against 566–584K in r2. With f and g built, S3 falls to 8–27% and the work spreads over the parts no hint spells out: the palette count (15–30%), the cross-edge exclusions and the invariant (34–46%), and the final count (2–16%).
  • With prompt R and no time limit, round 0 explores for a million characters instead of stopping. Neither attempt calls the problem open. Each runs to 1.0–1.1M characters, 95–100% off the map: 23–29% trying to prove L finite (the wrong answer), 17–32% on Schur-type colourings, 21% on lexicographic and tree colourings, and up to 12% on Shannon capacity and graph codes, against 2–3% on the standard bounds. s0 ends with a proof of L = ∞ that Sol finds wrong; s1 declines, saying it does not have the 2026 argument.
  • With prompt R, r1's thinking goes where it went before, for about four times as long. 64–65% of each attempt is on the proof, again mostly the maps f and g (33–35%) and the cross-edge rule (22%), in 1.3–1.6M characters against 353–374K. Neither calls the problem open. s1 also spends 17% on other invariants for the recursion and ends with a wrong proof (a false palette count); s0 never answers: its last 130K characters repeat "3+?   • With no time limit, both r2 attempts keep working on f and g and finish without them. 75–84% of each attempt goes on constructing f and g, over 1.1M characters (the standard attempts were cut at 566–584K). Both write up the whole construction in the last 1% of the trace and assume the lemma that r2 states without proof. Their thinking says so: s0 notes that its answer won't satisfy the instruction, and s1 decides to state the lemma without caveats ("This is circular but not flagged"). Sol marks both incomplete.
  • r3 works the same way under either prompt. Both prompt-R attempts prove the theorem, in 84–147K characters and 99–100% on the proof. f and g take 10–15%; most of the work goes on the cross edges and the invariant (47%) and the palette count (17–27%).

Where the thinking went

Each bar is one attempt's reasoning, split by what Sol says it was working on.

How this was made. Each attempt's reasoning, and its final answer where it gave one, was cut into windows of about 24,000 characters (433 in all). GPT-6.1 Sol (pro) read each window with the problem, the hint that attempt saw, the 12 claims of Astra's map of the reference proof and a catalogue of other approaches, and listed in order what the window works on, what it does with it (explores, establishes, uses the hint, doubts, abandons, writes up), its rough share of the window and a verbatim quote. These are one model's readings of very long traces, not ground truth.

A node counts as reached by an attempt when it gets at least 0.4% of the attempt's reasoning (at least 2,000 characters), or when the attempt establishes it, takes it from the hint or writes it up. The drawn walks skip passing mentions under 4,000 characters. With the standard prompt both r2 attempts were stopped at the one-hour limit, so their walks end mid-thought. With prompt R a stream that ended early was continued by a new request that resumes the reasoning where it stopped; a dashed tick on a timeline marks where a new request begins. Sol gave 123 names to work outside the catalogue in the prompt-R attempts (three in the others); the page groups 110 of the 126 into six nodes by their names (O7–O12) and leaves the rest under Other. Hint texts are exactly as Kimi saw them; a hint's token count is how many tokens it adds to Kimi's prompt.
