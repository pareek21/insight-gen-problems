Problem statement

For a positive integer $n$, write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. If $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ and $B\subseteq[n]$, let $x^B$ be obtained by flipping precisely the coordinates in $B$; abbreviate $x^{\{a\}}$ to $x^a$. For a total Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, define

$$s(f,x)=\bigl|\{a\in[n]:f(x^a)\ne f(x)\}\bigr|,\qquad s(f)=\max_x s(f,x).$$

Its block sensitivity $\operatorname{bs}(f,x)$ is the largest number of pairwise disjoint nonempty sets $B_1,\ldots,B_m\subseteq[n]$ such that $f(x^{B_j})\ne f(x)$ for every $j$, and $\operatorname{bs}(f)=\max_x\operatorname{bs}(f,x)$.

Determine whether there is a real constant $C>0$ such that

$$\operatorname{bs}(f)\le C\,s(f)^2$$

for every positive integer $n$ and every nonconstant total Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$. Prove your answer.

1 · Parameters and labels

Fix \(d\), put \(L=d+2,k=2M^2+1,r=\lceil\sqrt M\rceil\), and take \(M\) large. Label a regular tournament on \([k]\) by \(a(i,j)\in[r]\) so no sixteen-vertex set admits choices \(m_j\) with \(a(i,j)=m_j\) on all internal edges \(i\to j\). Choose labels independently and apply a union bound.

2 · Starting predicates and the construction

Keep \(H_\ell=d+1-\ell\) nested predicates.3 Initially use \(P_{0,q}=\mathbf1\{D\le H_0-q\}\), where \(D\) is distance to the nearest of \(M\) centres with disjoint supports of size \(L\). At level \(\ell\), use \(k\) rows of \(r\) children and set \(Q=H_{\ell-1}\)3. Define \(P_{\ell,q}\) as the OR of clauses indexed by rows \(i\): require \(P_{\ell-1,Q}(x_{i,c})=1\) for every \(c\), and \(P_{\ell-1,Q-q}(x_{j,a(i,j)})=0\) for every \(i\to j\).

3 · Profiles, recurrences, counting lemma

Track maxima \(S_{\ell,b}\) of one-sided sensitivity and \(J_{\ell,b}\) of flips changing two distinct predicates simultaneously from \(b\) to \(1-b\) on the same input; empty maxima are zero5. Initially \(S_{0,0}\le L,S_{0,1}\le LM,J_{0,b}=0\); the last fact uses that \(D\) is 1-Lipschitz4. Suppress the current level and prime the previous one. Establish \[ S_1\le M^2S'_0+rS'_1,\qquad J_1\le M^2J'_0+rS'_1, \] \[ S_0\le16S'_0+S'_1+3J'_1,\qquad J_0\le16S'_0+3J'_1. \] At a zero-valued input, repairable clauses fail exactly one condition. Labels bound target-failing clauses by fifteen. Gate-failing clauses induce a tournament of maximum outdegree one, so there are at most three and at most one sink. Repairing a nonsink’s gate changes two child predicates; for joint transitions even the sink must cross two thresholds.

4 · Ending: bounds, blocks, final OR, result

Normalize by \(M^{\ell+b}\). For fixed \(d\), taking \(M\) sufficiently large makes the recurrences give \(S_{\ell,b}\le2LM^{\ell+b}\) through depth \(d\). Extend support blocks by uniting corresponding copies across one row2: this gives \(Mk^\ell\) disjoint blocks changing every predicate from zero to one at the all-zero input2. Finally OR \(M\) depth-\(d\) copies. Its sensitivity is at most \(2LM^{d+1}\), its block sensitivity at least \(M^2k^d\), and the ratio at least \(2^d/(4L^2)\), which diverges.1

Only in the standard hint

  2. How the disjoint blocks are built, and that each one flips every predicate at the all-zero input. The map hint gives only their number.
  3. Level-by-level bookkeeping. Level \(\ell\) keeps \(d+1-\ell\) predicates, so one is used up per level and \(f\) is the last one left. The map hint gives one generic step and says “iterate \(d\) times”.
  4. Why the starting joint profiles are zero: the distance \(D\) is 1-Lipschitz.
  5. A convention: empty maxima count as zero.

1 · Parameters and labels

Fix \(d\), put \(L=d+2\), and take \(M\) large, \(k=2M^2+1\), \(r=\lceil\sqrt M\rceil\). Label a regular tournament on \([k]\) so no 16 vertices admit positions \(m_j\in[r]\) with \(a(i,j)=m_j\) on every internal edge \(i\to j\). Independent uniform labels work by the union bound \(k^{16}r^{-104}<1\)1.

2 · One step of the construction

Given nested \(P_1\ge\cdots\ge P_Q\), arrange disjoint children \(x_{j,c}\) in \(k\) rows of \(r\) positions. Define \(R_q\), \(1\le q<Q\), as the OR over \(i\) of \[ \bigwedge_c[P_Q(x_{i,c})=1]\ \wedge\ \bigwedge_{i\to j}[P_{Q-q}(x_{j,a(i,j)})=0]. \] Let \(S_b\) maximize ordinary one-sided sensitivity over the family, and \(J_b\) maximize the number of bits simultaneously flipping two distinct predicates from \(b\) to \(1-b\).

3 · Counting lemma and recurrences

At a zero, each repairable clause fails exactly one condition. Fewer than 16 candidates fail a target: their unique failed positions would otherwise violate the label property2. Gate-failing candidates induce outdegrees at most one, since every edge between them gives a failed gate3; hence there are at most three. Except possibly one sink, repairing a gate also destroys the strongest target in another candidate row, costing \(J_1\)4. For joint acceptance at \(q<q'\), use candidates at \(q\): every gate repair yielding acceptance at \(q'\) flips both child thresholds \(Q-q,Q-q'\)5, even at the sink. Writing primes for the resulting family gives \[ \begin{aligned} S'_0&\le16S_0+S_1+3J_1,&J'_0&\le16S_0+3J_1,\\ S'_1&\le M^2S_0+rS_1,&J'_1&\le M^2J_0+rS_1. \end{aligned} \] For transitions from one, prove the bounds by fixing a satisfied clause.6

4 · Starting predicates, iteration, blocks, final OR

Start with \(M\) disjoint weight-\(L\) centres on \(LM\) bits, and predicates for distance to the nearest centre at most \(d,d-1,\ldots,0\). Initially \(S_0\le L,S_1\le LM\) and \(J_0=J_1=0\). Iterate \(d\) times. Normalize level-\(\ell\) profiles by \(M^{\ell+b}\); for fixed \(d\), taking \(M\) sufficiently large keeps normalized ordinary profiles below \(2L\), since the normalized joint profiles tend to zero7. The initial \(M\) disjoint sensitive blocks at zero become \(Mk^\ell\) blocks at level \(\ell\). Finally OR together \(M\) final copies to balance the two one-sided sensitivities8.

  3. Why gate-failing clauses have out-degree at most one.
  4. Where the \(3J_1\) term comes from: repairing a gate also breaks a target in another candidate row.
  5. How joint transitions are counted: through the two thresholds \(Q-q\) and \(Q-q'\).
  6. How to prove the bounds for transitions from one.
  7. Why taking \(M\) large closes the recurrences.
  8. What the final OR is for.
