Where Kimi's thinking went, round by round

Every attempt's reasoning, cut into windows and labelled by GPT-6.1 Sol against the reference proof's 18 claims and a catalogue of approaches outside it, drawn as a walk over one shared map, one map per round of hints.

  • With no hint, Kimi recalls and counts. Each attempt spends 27–61% recalling the literature and 13–33% counting around a vertex of minimum out-degree; all four reach the minimal-counterexample reduction (6–14%) and call the problem open.
  • r1 and r2 hold the thinking at the reduction. Half of each attempt (50–59%) goes on the deletion step and the subset deficit, and r2's attempts prove the deficit. Three of the four then spend 5–16% trying to finish from the deficit directly, by iterating neighbourhoods or breadth-first layers, and call the problem open. No attempt gets past the deficit.
  • r3 moves the frontier to the pruning step. 82–86% of each attempt is on the proof. Both take the blow-up (12–20%) and the extremal pair, and the largest single node in each is the pruning step (23–25%), which both try to reconstruct and then call unproved. The pruning lemma's own eight claims are barely touched (s1: 4%, on the conflict graph).
  • r4 puts the pruning lemma at the centre. With the lemma and its matching argument stated, 88–94% of each attempt is on the proof and 48–73% on the lemma's claims. s0 writes a full proof that breaks at the rank bound dim ker A ≥ k, the one step r4 leaves out; s1 calls the problem open.
  • With the rank bound (r5), two of four attempts prove the theorem. Both correct proofs (s0, s3) reach all 18 claims, with 91–99% on the proof and 56–59% on the pruning lemma. The two failures spend as much on the lemma (52–65%), and s2 reaches 17 of the 18 claims, but both call the matching and kernel estimates unproved and the problem open.
  • The hinted deletion step keeps a fifth to a third of every attempt in r1–r3 (21–35%): Kimi re-derives it at length rather than taking it from the hint. Only once the pruning lemma takes over (r4–r5) does it fall to 6–15%.

Where the thinking went

Each bar is one attempt's reasoning, split by what Sol says it was working on.

How this was made. Each attempt's reasoning, and its final answer where it gave one, was cut into windows of about 24,000 characters (163 in all). GPT-6.1 Sol (pro) read each window with the problem, the hint that attempt saw, the 18 claims of Astra's map of the reference proof and a catalogue of other approaches, and listed in order what the window works on, what it does with it (explores, establishes, uses the hint, doubts, abandons, writes up), its rough share of the window and a verbatim quote. These are one model's readings of very long traces, not ground truth.

A node counts as reached by an attempt when it gets at least 0.4% of the attempt's reasoning (at least 2,000 characters), or when the attempt establishes it, takes it from the hint or writes it up. The drawn walks skip passing mentions under 4,000 characters. Hint texts are exactly as Kimi saw them; a hint's token count is how many tokens it adds to Kimi's prompt.
