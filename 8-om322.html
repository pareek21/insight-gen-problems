#322 Tingley

Tingley's problem (1987). OpenAI release, Sept 2026. True.
Reference proof: 8,427 tokens. Statement: the definitions and theorem from OpenAI's math release (commit 1581d4c, Apache-2.0), posed as “Determine whether …” so that the answer isn't given away.

Problem

For a real normed space $X$, write $S_X=\{x\in X:\lVert x\rVert=1\}$ for its unit sphere, with the distance inherited from $X$.

Let $X$ and $Y$ be nonzero real Banach spaces. The spaces may have arbitrary dimension and need not be separable, reflexive, smooth, or strictly convex.

Determine whether every surjective isometry $f:S_X\to S_Y$ extends to a surjective real-linear isometry of the spaces, that is, whether there is always a surjective real-linear isometry $T:X\to Y$ with $T(x)=f(x)$ for every $x\in S_X$. Prove your answer.

Align extremal chords using a continuous return map that contracts noncompactness, not necessarily distance. Let \(M\) be the supremum of \(|\|f(x)-qf(y)\|-\|x-qy\||\) for unit \(x,y\) and \(0\le q\le1\). If \(M>0\), inversion and an ultrapower let you assume a positive maximum at \((x_0,y,t)\), \(0<t<1\), while retaining the global bound.

From \(ty\), follow a unit direction \(v\) to the sphere, map its endpoint by \(f\), take the opposite contact on the line through \(tf(y)\), and pull back to \(z\). Denote the four successive radii by \(p,s,u,r\), and set \(g(v)=(ty-z)/r\).

On \(E=\{v:s-p=M\}\), maximality forces \(r-u=M\) and a common support for \(v,g(v)\). Crucially, every unit direction sharing a support with \(g(v)\) lies in \(E\). Iterating strictly positive convex combinations builds a nonempty closed convex \(C\subset E\) invariant under \(g\).

All radii lie in \([1-t,1+t]\). Comparing two directions and partitioning their scalar radii into small intervals gives \[ \chi(g(H))\le(1-M/(1+t))^2\chi(H)\qquad(H\subset C), \] where \(\chi\) is Kuratowski noncompactness. Darbo's theorem provides \(g(v)=v\), aligning chords with \(s-p=r-u=M\) and \(p+r=s+u\le2\).

Compare defects at two radii so one reaches \(0\) or \(1\), cancel the common target norm, and repeat for \(f^{-1}\). Supporting-functional signs and convexity then rule out this aligned configuration. Thus \(M=0\), and the radial extension is an onto isometry; apply Mazur–Ulam.

Set \(\epsilon=1-t,\ A=p/d,\ B=u/d\); then \(A+B<1\). Take \(h=\min(t/s,\epsilon/u)\). Weight the signed defect at \(t-sh\) by \(u\), and the negative signed defect at \(t+uh\) by \(s\). Supporting the resulting source secant gives, for every support \(\varphi\) at \(v+(hs/p)y\), \[ \epsilon/p\le\varphi(v)\le \begin{cases}1-B,&B\le\epsilon,\\ B,&B>\epsilon.\end{cases} \]

The uniform Lipschitz dependence on \(q\) and the identities \(D_0=D_1=0\) ensure \(0<t<1\).

Fix \(b=f(y)\). For each unit direction \(v\), define successive radial contacts by \[ x=ty+pv,\qquad f(x)=tb+sw,\qquad f(z)=tb-uw, \] and put \(r=\|z-ty\|\), \(g(v)=(ty-z)/r\). All radii lie in \([1-t,1+t]\). On \(E=\{v:s-p=M\}\), maximality forces \[ r-u=M,\qquad \|pv+rg(v)\|=p+r. \] Thus \(v,g(v)\) share a support; moreover, every unit direction sharing a support with \(g(v)\) belongs to \(E\).

Build a closed convex set \(C\subset E\) by repeatedly adjoining \(g(a_n)\), where \(a_n\) are strictly positive convex combinations of earlier directions, chosen densely in their eventual closed convex hull. Common supports keep the hull inside \(E\); continuity makes it invariant. Comparing return directions and partitioning according to the bounded radii \(p,u\) gives \[ \chi(g(H))\le(1-M/(1+t))^2\chi(H),\qquad H\subset C, \] where \(\chi\) is the infimum of diameter bounds for finite covers. Darbo’s theorem gives a fixed point. The chords now align, with \[ z=ty-rv,\qquad s-p=r-u=M,\qquad d=p+r=s+u\le2. \]

Set \(\epsilon=1-t\), \(A=p/d\), \(B=u/d\), so \(A+B<1\). Put \(h=\min(t/s,\epsilon/u)\), \(\theta=hs/p\). Weight the defects \(D_{t-sh}(x,y)\) and \(-D_{t+uh}(z,y)\) by \(u,s\), respectively; their target norms cancel, and one defect is zero. Use the resulting secant bound and supporting functionals to prove that every support \(\varphi\) at \(v+\theta y\) satisfies \[ \frac{\epsilon}{p}\le\varphi(v)\le \begin{cases} 1-B,&B\le\epsilon,\\ B,&B>\epsilon. \end{cases} \]

If \(B>\epsilon\), this yields \(pu\ge d\epsilon\) and forces every support at \(v\) to be negative on \(y\). Consequently \(u<1\) and \(A>\epsilon\). Apply the same argument to the inverse map with first endpoint \(f(z)\); this swaps \(A,B\). Thus either large case gives both inequalities and makes every support at \(w\) positive on \(b\). For sufficiently small \(\eta>0\), \[ \|sw+\eta b\|>s,\qquad \|pv+\eta y\|<p, \] contradicting the defect bound at radius \(t-\eta\).

Otherwise \(A,B\le\epsilon\). Applying the support estimate in both directions gives \[ \epsilon\le d\min\{A(1-B),B(1-A)\}<d/4\le1/2. \] Hence \(p/t<r/\epsilon\) and \(u/\epsilon\le s/t\). For the convex functions \(F(\lambda)=\|y+\lambda v\|\), \(G(\lambda)=\|b+\lambda w\|\), use \[ F(0)=G(0)=1,\qquad F(p/t)=G(s/t)=1/t \] to obtain \(F(r/\epsilon)>1/t\ge G(u/\epsilon)\). This contradicts \(\|y-z\|=\|b-f(z)\|\).

Therefore \(M=0\). Scaling now makes the radial extension an onto isometry fixing zero; apply Mazur–Ulam.
