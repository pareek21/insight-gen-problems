#132 Sensitivity vs block sensitivity

Question of Nisan and Szegedy (1994). OpenAI release, Sept 2026. False: a superquadratic separation.
Reference proof: 6,442 tokens. Statement: the definitions and theorem from OpenAI's math release (commit 1581d4c, Apache-2.0), posed as “Determine whether …” so that the answer isn't given away.

Problem

For a positive integer $n$, write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. If $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ and $B\subseteq[n]$, let $x^B$ be obtained by flipping precisely the coordinates in $B$; abbreviate $x^{\{a\}}$ to $x^a$. For a total Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, define

$$s(f,x)=\bigl|\{a\in[n]:f(x^a)\ne f(x)\}\bigr|,\qquad s(f)=\max_x s(f,x).$$

Its block sensitivity $\operatorname{bs}(f,x)$ is the largest number of pairwise disjoint nonempty sets $B_1,\ldots,B_m\subseteq[n]$ such that $f(x^{B_j})\ne f(x)$ for every $j$, and $\operatorname{bs}(f)=\max_x\operatorname{bs}(f,x)$.

Determine whether there is a real constant $C>0$ such that

$$\operatorname{bs}(f)\le C\,s(f)^2$$

for every positive integer $n$ and every nonconstant total Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$. Prove your answer.

Recurse using a regular tournament on \(2M^2+1\) rows of \(\lceil\sqrt M\rceil\) children. Track joint sensitivity of nested predicates: bits simultaneously changing two distinct predicates from \(b\) to \(1-b\). Start with unions of Hamming balls of consecutive radii \(d,\ldots,0\) around indicators of \(M\) disjoint blocks of size \(L=d+2\). Their joint sensitivities vanish.

Iterate a tournament construction with \(k=2M^2+1\) rows and \(r=\lceil\sqrt M\rceil\) children per row. Each clause requires its row’s strongest child predicates to be one and selected weaker predicates in outgoing neighbors to be zero. Choose edge labels so no 16 rows have all internal incoming labels constant at each vertex.

For zero-to-one flips, consider clauses failing exactly one condition: fewer than 16 can fail one target, and at most three can fail one gate. Crucially, all but at most one gate-repair clause force two child thresholds to change together. Keep these costs in the joint profile rather than ordinary sensitivity.

With \(M\) large relative to depth \(d\), aim to bound level-\(\ell\) sensitivities at value \(b\) by \(2LM^{\ell+b}\), while obtaining \(Mk^\ell\) disjoint sensitive blocks at zero. OR together \(M\) depth-\(d\) copies: the ratio is then at least \(2^d/[4(d+2)^2]\), hence unbounded.

Standard 5%

294 tokens (4.6% of the proof) · N 0/4 · K 0/4

Track joint sensitivity of nested predicates, not just ordinary sensitivity: a joint transition flips two distinct predicates in the same direction on the same input, using the same coordinate. Start with unions of balls of radii \(0,\ldots,d\) about \(M\) strings with disjoint supports of size \(L=d+2\); all joint sensitivities are zero because distance to the nearest centre changes by at most one along an edge.

Recursively arrange children in \(k=2M^2+1\) tournament rows, with \(r=\lceil\sqrt M\rceil\) children per row. Each clause requires its row’s strongest predicates to be one and a weaker predicate in one labelled child of each outgoing row to be zero. Random labels limit clauses repairable through a target to fewer than sixteen. At most three clauses are repairable through a gate; all but possibly one require a joint child transition.

For fixed depth \(d\) and sufficiently large \(M\), seek one-sided sensitivities bounded by \(2LM^d,2LM^{d+1}\), together with \(Mk^d\) disjoint sensitive blocks at zero. Taking an OR of \(M\) copies then yields \[ s(f)\le2LM^{d+1},\qquad \operatorname{bs}(f)\ge M^2k^d. \] The ratio is therefore at least \(2^d/[4(d+2)^2]\), which diverges.

Fix \(d\), put \(L=d+2,k=2M^2+1,r=\lceil\sqrt M\rceil\), and take \(M\) large. Label a regular tournament on \([k]\) by \(a(i,j)\in[r]\) so no sixteen-vertex set admits choices \(m_j\) with \(a(i,j)=m_j\) on all internal edges \(i\to j\). Choose labels independently and apply a union bound.

Keep \(H_\ell=d+1-\ell\) nested predicates. Initially use \(P_{0,q}=\mathbf1\{D\le H_0-q\}\), where \(D\) is distance to the nearest of \(M\) centres with disjoint supports of size \(L\). At level \(\ell\), use \(k\) rows of \(r\) children and set \(Q=H_{\ell-1}\). Define \(P_{\ell,q}\) as the OR of clauses indexed by rows \(i\): require \(P_{\ell-1,Q}(x_{i,c})=1\) for every \(c\), and \(P_{\ell-1,Q-q}(x_{j,a(i,j)})=0\) for every \(i\to j\).

Track maxima \(S_{\ell,b}\) of one-sided sensitivity and \(J_{\ell,b}\) of flips changing two distinct predicates simultaneously from \(b\) to \(1-b\) on the same input; empty maxima are zero. Initially \(S_{0,0}\le L,S_{0,1}\le LM,J_{0,b}=0\); the last fact uses that \(D\) is 1-Lipschitz. Suppress the current level and prime the previous one. Establish \[ S_1\le M^2S'_0+rS'_1,\qquad J_1\le M^2J'_0+rS'_1, \] \[ S_0\le16S'_0+S'_1+3J'_1,\qquad J_0\le16S'_0+3J'_1. \] At a zero-valued input, repairable clauses fail exactly one condition. Labels bound target-failing clauses by fifteen. Gate-failing clauses induce a tournament of maximum outdegree one, so there are at most three and at most one sink. Repairing a nonsink’s gate changes two child predicates; for joint transitions even the sink must cross two thresholds.

Normalize by \(M^{\ell+b}\). For fixed \(d\), taking \(M\) sufficiently large makes the recurrences give \(S_{\ell,b}\le2LM^{\ell+b}\) through depth \(d\). Extend support blocks by uniting corresponding copies across one row: this gives \(Mk^\ell\) disjoint blocks changing every predicate from zero to one at the all-zero input. Finally OR \(M\) depth-\(d\) copies. Its sensitivity is at most \(2LM^{d+1}\), its block sensitivity at least \(M^2k^d\), and the ratio at least \(2^d/(4L^2)\), which diverges.

Use nested predicates and track \(J_{\ell,b}\), the maximum number of bits simultaneously changing two distinct predicates from \(b\) to \(1-b\), alongside ordinary one-sided profiles \(S_{\ell,b}\). Both maxima range over relevant inputs and predicate indices.

Fix \(d\), and put \(L=d+2,\ k=2M^2+1,\ r=\lceil\sqrt M\rceil\). Initialize \(P_{0,q}\), for \(1\le q\le d+1\), as unions of radius-\((d+1-q)\) balls around indicators of \(M\) disjoint \(L\)-bit blocks. Their profiles satisfy \(S_{0,0}\le L,S_{0,1}\le LM,J_{0,b}=0\).

Choose a regular tournament on \(k\) rows, each with \(r\) children. Label edges by \(a(i,j)\in[r]\), forbidding 16 vertices with labels constant on internal edges entering each vertex. Independent uniform labels suffice: the failure probability is at most \(k^{16}r^{-104}<1\) for \(M\ge3\).

At level \(\ell\), put \(Q=d+2-\ell\). For \(1\le q<Q\), let \(P_{\ell,q}\) be the OR over rows \(i\) of clauses requiring \[ P_{\ell-1,Q}(x_{i,c})=1\quad(c\in[r]),\qquad P_{\ell-1,Q-q}(x_{j,a(i,j)})=0\quad(i\to j). \] The predicates remain nested.

At a zero, candidate clauses fail exactly one condition. Labels bound one-target failures by 15. One-gate failures induce outdegrees at most one: at most three clauses, with at most one sink. Nonsink gate repairs change two child predicates—the gate and strongest predicates. Joint parent transitions force two child thresholds to change even for the sink.

Writing primes for previous-level profiles, establish \[ \begin{aligned} S_{\ell,1}&\le M^2S'_0+rS'_1,&J_{\ell,1}&\le M^2J'_0+rS'_1,\\ S_{\ell,0}&\le16S'_0+S'_1+3J'_1,&J_{\ell,0}&\le16S'_0+3J'_1. \end{aligned} \] For the one-side bounds, fix a satisfied clause and count flips breaking it.

After division by \(M^{\ell+b}\), joint profiles tend to zero as \(M\to\infty\) at fixed depth, while ordinary profiles have limit superior at most \(L\). Thus sufficiently large \(M\) gives \(S_{\ell,b}\le2LM^{\ell+b}\) through depth \(d\).

Joining matching child blocks across each row yields \(Mk^\ell\) disjoint sensitive blocks at zero. OR together \(M\) depth-\(d\) copies. Then \(s\le2LM^{d+1}\), \(\operatorname{bs}\ge M^2k^d\), and the ratio is at least \(2^d/[4(d+2)^2]\).
