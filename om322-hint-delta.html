Problem statement

For a real normed space $X$, write $S_X=\{x\in X:\lVert x\rVert=1\}$ for its unit sphere, with the distance inherited from $X$.

Let $X$ and $Y$ be nonzero real Banach spaces. The spaces may have arbitrary dimension and need not be separable, reflexive, smooth, or strictly convex.

Determine whether every surjective isometry $f:S_X\to S_Y$ extends to a surjective real-linear isometry of the spaces, that is, whether there is always a surjective real-linear isometry $T:X\to Y$ with $T(x)=f(x)$ for every $x\in S_X$. Prove your answer.

1 · Strategy, defect, attainment

Align extremal chords using a continuous return map that contracts noncompactness.1 Let \(M=\sup|\|f(x)-qf(y)\|-\|x-qy\||\), over unit \(x,y\) and \(0\le q\le1\). If \(M>0\), inversion and an ultrapower attain a positive maximum at \((x_0,y,t)\), \(0<t<1\), retaining the global bound.

2 · Return map, support propagation, invariant set

Put \(b=f(y)\). For a unit direction \(v\), construct successive sphere contacts \[ x=ty+pv,\qquad f(x)=tb+sw,\qquad f(z)=tb-uw, \] with \(w\in S_Y\), and set \(r=\|ty-z\|\), \(g(v)=(ty-z)/r\). All radii lie in \([1-t,1+t]\).2 On \(E=\{v:s-p=M\}\), maximality forces \(r-u=M\) and2 a common support for \(v,g(v)\). Every unit direction sharing a support with \(g(v)\) lies in \(E\). Use this to build a nonempty closed convex invariant \(C\subset E\): repeatedly adjoin \(g(a_n)\), where \(a_n\) is a strictly positive convex combination of previous directions, arranging the \(a_n\) dense in \(C\). Strict positivity makes a support at \(a_n,g(a_n)\) support all preceding directions.

3 · Contraction and Darbo

Comparing directions gives coefficient \(up/(rs)\le\gamma=(1-M/(1+t))^2<1\), with errors controlled by scalar differences of \(p,u\). Partition those ranges into small intervals3 to obtain \(\chi(g(H))\le\gamma\chi(H)\) for \(H\subset C\), where \(\chi\) is Kuratowski noncompactness. Darbo yields \(g(v)=v\), hence aligned chords with \(s-p=r-u=M\) and \(d=p+r=s+u\le2\)4.

4 · The secant estimate

Set \(\epsilon=1-t,\ A=p/d,\ B=u/d\); then \(A+B<1\).4 Take \(h=\min(t/s,\epsilon/u)\). Weight the signed defect at \(t-sh\) by \(u\), and the negative signed defect at \(t+uh\) by \(s\). One vanishes, while the common target norm cancels. Supporting the resulting source secant gives, for every support \(\varphi\) at \(v+(hs/p)y\), 5\[ \epsilon/p\le\varphi(v)\le \begin{cases}1-B,&B\le\epsilon,\\ B,&B>\epsilon.\end{cases} \]

5 · Large ratios excluded

If \(B>\epsilon\), deduce that every support at \(v\) is negative on \(y\), that \(u<r<1\), and that \(pu\ge d\epsilon\); hence \(A>\epsilon\). The inverse configuration interchanges \(A,B\), so either strict inequality also forces every support at \(w\) to be positive on \(b\). Decreasing \(t\) slightly increases the target norm and decreases the source norm, contradicting maximality. For the source decrease, use compactness of supports on \(\operatorname{span}\{v,y\}\).6

6 · Ratio inequalities, contradiction, result

Otherwise the two configurations give \(\epsilon\le dA(1-B)\) and \(\epsilon\le dB(1-A)\). Since \(A+B<1\) and \(d\le2\), infer \(\epsilon<1/2\).7 Now \(p/t<r/\epsilon\) and \(u/\epsilon\le s/t\). Starting from the unit endpoint identities, convexity gives \[ \|y+(r/\epsilon)v\|>1/t\ge\|b+(u/\epsilon)w\|, \] contradicting preservation of \(\|y-z\|\). Thus \(M=0\); scaling gives an onto radial isometry, and Mazur–Ulam gives real linearity.

Round 1 · 168 tokens · answer, defect, ultrapower

The answer is yes.1 For \(a,c\in S_X\) and \(0\le q\le1\) put \(D_q(a,c)=\|f(a)-qf(c)\|-\|a-qc\|\) and \(M=\sup|D_q(a,c)|\): if \(M=0\), the radial map \(T(x)=\|x\|f(x/\|x\|)\) is a surjective isometry fixing \(0\), hence linear by Mazur–Ulam. To rule out \(M>0\), pass to ultrapowers to get a surjective sphere isometry with the same defect bound \(M\) at which it is attained, \(D_t(x_0,y)=M\) for some \(x_0,y\) and \(0<t<1\).

Round 2 · +212 · return map, invariant set, contraction factor, Darbo

With \(b=f(y)\), use a return map: for a unit direction \(v\) take the sphere point \(x=ty+pv\) (\(p>0\)), write \(f(x)=tb+sw\), take the opposite sphere point \(f(z)=tb-uw\) on the line through \(tb\), and set \(g(v)=(ty-z)/\|ty-z\|\). On \(E=\{v:s-p=M\}\), \(v\) and \(g(v)\) share a supporting functional, which yields a nonempty closed convex \(C\subset E\) with \(g(C)\subset C\) on which \(g\) shrinks the Kuratowski measure of noncompactness by the factor \((1-M/(1+t))^2<1\); Darbo's theorem then gives aligned chords \(x=ty+pv\), \(z=ty-rv\), \(f(x)=tb+sw\), \(f(z)=tb-uw\)4 with \(s-p=r-u=M\).

Round 3 · +321 · how to build the set, the final comparison

The set \(C\) comes from a stronger fact:4 if \(v\in E\) and a unit \(l\) shares a supporting functional with \(g(v)\), then \(l\in E\), since the \(z,r,u\) of \(v\) give \(p(l)+r=\|x(l)-z\|\le s(l)+u\)2; so from \(v_0\in E\) set \(v_{n+1}=g(a_n)\), with \(a_n\) a strictly positive convex combination of \(v_0,\dots,v_n\) chosen so that the \(a_n\) are dense in \(C=\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\{v_n\}\), and a common support of \(a_n\) and \(v_{n+1}\) equals 1 on every \(v_i\), which keeps each hull inside \(E\). The aligned chords are contradictory: with \(\epsilon=1-t\), the convex profiles \(F(\lambda)=\|y+\lambda v\|\) and \(G(\lambda)=\|b+\lambda w\|\) satisfy \(F(0)=G(0)=1\), \(F(p/t)=G(s/t)=1/t\) and \(\epsilon F(r/\epsilon)=\|y-z\|=\|b-f(z)\|=\epsilon G(u/\epsilon)\)3, so it suffices to show \(p/t<r/\epsilon\) and \(u/\epsilon\le s/t\), which follow from the defect bound at nearby radii applied to \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\).
