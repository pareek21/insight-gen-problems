Where Kimi's thinking went, round by round

Every attempt's reasoning, cut into windows and labelled by GPT-6.1 Sol against the reference proof's 14 claims and a catalogue of approaches outside it, drawn as a walk over one shared map, one map per round of hints.

  • Without the construction, none of the thinking is on the proof. With no hint, each attempt spends 22–69% on the quartic upper bound through degree and 20–37% recalling the literature; none looks for a superquadratic separation. Told the answer (r1), both try plain composition (25–49%) and gadgets of their own (27–35%), and 97–99% stays off the map.
  • The nested predicates (r2) move the thinking onto the proof. 36–70% of each attempt is on the proof's claims, mostly the sensitivity count. Both establish the blocks at zero, but with no base predicates and no way to count sensitivity, both call the problem open.
  • With the base and the clause count (r3), 88–89% is on the proof and both attempts write a full proof. They reach 12–13 of the 14 claims and put 48–57% into the sensitivity count, 15–20% on the joint bound alone. That is where both proofs break: s0 reindexes the predicates and lets a gate test the target predicate itself; s1 drops the joint-sensitivity term.
  • With the gate rule and the zero-side recurrence (r4), one of four attempts proves the theorem. 82–96% of each attempt is on the proof and 47–62% on the sensitivity count. The correct proof (s1) is the most focused: 96% on the proof, 55% on the count and 24% on the recurrences. The failures stay in the bookkeeping the hint leaves out: s0 and s3 spend 32% on the bounds at output one and break at joint sensitivity there; s2 reaches 13 of the 14 claims and still calls the problem open.
  • The thinking grows with the hint, then levels off. About 100–220K characters per attempt up to r1, 350K in r2, 510–550K in r3 and 370–480K in r4, all within the hour.

Where the thinking went

Each bar is one attempt's reasoning, split by what Sol says it was working on.

How this was made. Each attempt's reasoning, and its final answer where it gave one, was cut into windows of about 24,000 characters (192 in all). GPT-6.1 Sol (pro) read each window with the problem, the hint that attempt saw, the 14 claims of Astra's map of the reference proof and a catalogue of other approaches, and listed in order what the window works on, what it does with it (explores, establishes, uses the hint, doubts, abandons, writes up), its rough share of the window and a verbatim quote. These are one model's readings of very long traces, not ground truth.

A node counts as reached by an attempt when it gets at least 0.4% of the attempt's reasoning (at least 2,000 characters), or when the attempt establishes it, takes it from the hint or writes it up. The drawn walks skip passing mentions under 4,000 characters. Hint texts are exactly as Kimi saw them; a hint's token count is how many tokens it adds to Kimi's prompt.
