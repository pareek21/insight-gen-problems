Problem statement

A polynomial threshold function assigns a sign to each vertex of the Boolean cube by evaluating a real polynomial. On the cube, the identities $x_i^2=1$ allow every polynomial to be replaced by a multilinear polynomial of no larger degree without changing its values. Average sensitivity measures the expected number of coordinate changes that reverse that sign. For $x\in\{-1,1\}^n$, let $x^{\oplus i}$ be obtained by reversing coordinate $i$. For $f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$, define

$$I(f)=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb P\{f(X)\ne f(X^{\oplus i})\},\qquad X\text{ uniform on }\{-1,1\}^n.$$

This quantity is also called the total influence of $f$. Throughout,

$$\operatorname{sgn}(t)=\begin{cases}1,&t\ge0,\\-1,&t<0.\end{cases}$$

Determine whether there is an absolute constant $C>0$ with the following property: for all integers $n\ge1$ and $1\le d\le n$, and every real multilinear polynomial $p$ in $n$ variables of degree at most $d$, the function $f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$ defined by $f(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(p(x))$, with $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=1$, satisfies

$$I(f)\le C\,d\sqrt n.$$

The polynomial may vanish on the cube, and no regularity condition is imposed on $p$. Prove your answer.

Remove zeros by a small positive constant perturbation.1 In counting inner product, let \(\Pi_k\) be the successive orthogonal layer projections of \(\sqrt{|p|}\,V_k\)2, where \(V_k\) is the Fourier degree-\(\le k\) space. Set \(M=\sum_k(k-n/2)\Pi_k\) and \(H=\operatorname{diag}((\prod_i x_i)f)\)2.

Prove the edge-recovery bound \(I(f)\le2^{1-n}\|MH+HM\|_{\rm HS}^2\). The masses \(2^{-n}\|\Pi_sH\Pi_r\|_{\rm HS}^2\) couple binomial marginals \(R,S\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/2)\), with \(R+S\ge n-d\); their moment \(\mathbb E(R+S-n)^2\) is the normalized anticommutator energy.3

Reflect one index and use this one-sided coupling lemma: if \(U,V\) have the same law, standard deviation \(\sigma\), and \(V-U\le a\), then \(\mathbb E(U-V)^2\le8a\sigma\). Take \(U=S,V=n-R,a=d\).4

  2. Which weight and which sign to use: \(|p|\) and \(\chi f\). The map hint states its lemma for any weight and any sign.
  3. The binomial coupling. The block masses of \(H\) couple two \(\mathrm{Bin}(n,\tfrac12)\) laws with \(R+S\ge n-d\), and their second moment is the anticommutator energy.

Map 5%

Astra, from its map of the proof's claims

0/4 correct311 tokens · 5.6%attempts averaged 74K tokens

Let $V_k$ be the Fourier degree-at-most-$k$ space, with $V_{-1}=0$. In the counting inner product set $E_k=\sqrt wV_k\ominus\sqrt wV_{k-1}$ for any $w>0$1, with projections $\Pi_k$ and $M=\sum_k(k-n/2)\Pi_k$. Aim to prove that any sign multiplier1 $H$ satisfies

\[ \#\{\text{ordered equal-sign edges}\}\le2\|MH+HM\|_{\rm HS}^2. \]

Coordinate multiplication $Z_i$ is grade-tridiagonal. With $J=\sum_k(-1)^k\Pi_k$ and $W=\sum_k\mathrm i^k\Pi_k$,

\[ W(Z_i-JZ_iJ)W^*/2=\mathrm i[M,Z_i]. \]

This proves $\|[M,Z_i]\|_{\rm op}\le1$, hence $|M_{xy}|\le1/2$ off the diagonal.2 Tridiagonality and the grade dimensions also give

\[ \sum_{x,y}\rho(x,y)|M_{xy}|^2\le\|M\|_{\rm HS}^2=n2^n/4, \]

where $\rho$ is Hamming distance. Thus the nonnegative neighbor deficits $1/4-|M_{xy}|^2$ sum to at most twice the diagonal energy. The anticommutator detects diagonal energy as well as equal-sign edge energy.3

Only in this hint, not in standard

  1. The general form: the grading for any weight \(w\) and the edge bound for any sign multiplier \(H\). It never says to take \(w=|p|\) or \(H=\chi f\).
  2. The commutator bound and its proof: \(Z_i\) is tridiagonal in the grading, and conjugating by \(J\) and \(W\) gives \(\|[M,Z_i]\|\le1\), so \(|M_{xy}|\le\tfrac12\).
  3. The energy count that turns this into the edge bound: Hamming-weighted energy at most \(\|M\|_{\rm HS}^2\), edge deficits at most twice the diagonal energy, and the anticommutator sees both.

The answer is yes, with \(C=8\), by an exact linear-algebra argument rather than regularity or invariance.1 After making \(p\) nonzero on the cube, multiply by the full parity \(\chi(x)=\prod_i x_i\): the sensitive edges of \(f\) are exactly the edges on which \(h=\chi f\) is constant2, and \(q=\chi p=h\,|p|\) has Fourier support only in degrees at least \(n-d\)3. Then orthogonalise the degree filtration in the weight \(|p|\): with \(V_k\) the functions of degree at most \(k\), let \(\Pi_k\) project (counting inner product) onto \(\sqrt{|p|}V_k\ominus\sqrt{|p|}V_{k-1}\); multiplication by \(h\) then maps grade \(r\) only into grades \(s\ge n-d-r\). For \(M=\sum_k(k-\frac n2)\Pi_k\), prove \(\|[M,Z_i]\|\le1\) (\(Z_i\): multiplication by \(x_i\))4 and deduce that the ordered neighbours with \(h(x)=h(y)\) number at most \(2\|MH+HM\|_{\rm HS}^2\).

Only in this hint, not in standard

  1. The answer and the kind of argument: yes, \(C=8\), by exact linear algebra rather than regularity or invariance.
  2. Why the parity twist helps: the sensitive edges of \(f\) are exactly the edges where \(h=\chi f\) is constant.
  3. Why the degree matters: \(q=\chi p\) lives in degrees \(\ge n-d\). The standard hint uses this only through \(R+S\ge n-d\).
  4. The key tool for the edge bound: \(\|[M,Z_i]\|\le1\) for the coordinate multipliers.
