Where Kimi's thinking went, round by round

Every attempt's reasoning, cut into windows and labelled by GPT-6.1 Sol against the reference proof's 11 claims and a catalogue of approaches outside it, drawn as a walk over one shared map, one map per round of hints.

  • With no hint, all four attempts decline, and none of the thinking touches the proof. In 3–31K characters, 37–82% goes on the statement's premise that a proof exists (whether it can be used, and how to decline honestly) and 10–35% on the conjecture's status. s1 alone tries constructions, edge doubling (32%) and covers from flows (20%), before declining.
  • r1 moves the thinking onto the gluing, and both attempts stop there. 60–62% of each attempt is on the proof, 39–42% on the gluing claims. Both prove that consistent label pairs give the cover (S7) and test the gluing on small examples (12–14%), but neither finds the argument that the translations exist: both doubt it (6–13%) and decline. Neither touches the reduction to cubic graphs.
  • With the linear system, its dual and the parity identity (r2), three of four attempts prove the theorem. 66–90% of each attempt is on the proof. All four also work out the reduction to cubic graphs (9–23%), which no hint mentions, and the correct proofs reach 9–10 of the 11 claims.
  • Even with the proof in hand, the premise costs time. s0, s2 and s3 spend 15–26% of r2 deciding whether the premise lets them answer at all; s3 ends by declining, calling the parity identity unestablished. s1 instead spends 30% trying to prove Jaeger's 8-flow theorem itself, which the hint cites.
  • Unlike Goemans and Ramsey, the thinking grows with the hint: 3–31K characters with no hint, 89–164K in r1 and 154–367K in r2.

Where the thinking went

Each bar is one attempt's reasoning, split by what Sol says it was working on.

How this was made. Each attempt's reasoning, and its final answer where it gave one, was cut into windows of about 24,000 characters (59 in all). GPT-6.1 Sol (pro) read each window with the problem, the hint that attempt saw, the 11 claims of Astra's map of the reference proof and a catalogue of other approaches, and listed in order what the window works on, what it does with it (explores, establishes, uses the hint, doubts, abandons, writes up), its rough share of the window and a verbatim quote. These are one model's readings of very long traces, not ground truth.

A node counts as reached by an attempt when it gets at least 0.4% of the attempt's reasoning (at least 2,000 characters), or when the attempt establishes it, takes it from the hint or writes it up. The drawn walks skip passing mentions under 4,000 characters. Hint texts are exactly as Kimi saw them; a hint's token count is how many tokens it adds to Kimi's prompt.
