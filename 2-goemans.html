Goemans

Goemans' conjecture (1999). Disproved July 2026 (Rybin; verified in Isabelle). False: the answer is a counterexample.
Reference proof: 1,254 tokens. Statement written for this study from the conjecture as stated by Traub, Vargas Koch and Zenklusen (arXiv:2308.02651), posed as “prove or disprove”.

Problem

Single-source unsplittable flow. Let $G = (V, A)$ be a finite directed graph with a source $s \in V$ and terminals $t_1, \dots, t_k \in V \setminus \{s\}$ (distinct), where terminal $t_i$ has a demand $d_i > 0$. Let $d_{\max} = \max_i d_i$.

  • A flow is a vector $x \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{A}$ such that for every vertex $v$, the total value on arcs leaving $v$ minus the total value on arcs entering $v$ equals $\sum_i d_i$ if $v = s$, equals $-d_i$ if $v = t_i$, and equals $0$ otherwise.
  • An unsplittable flow $\mathcal{P}$ chooses one directed path $P_i$ from $s$ to $t_i$ for each $i$. Its load on an arc $a$ is $\mathrm{flow}_{\mathcal{P}}(a) = \sum_{i:\ a \in P_i} d_i$.
  • Given arc costs $c \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{A}$, the cost of $x$ is $\sum_{a \in A} c(a)\,x(a)$, and the cost of $\mathcal{P}$ is $\sum_{a \in A} c(a)\,\mathrm{flow}_{\mathcal{P}}(a)$.

Dinitz, Garg and Goemans proved that for every such instance and every flow $x$, there is an unsplittable flow $\mathcal{P}$ with $\mathrm{flow}_{\mathcal{P}}(a) \le x(a) + d_{\max}$ for every arc $a$.

Prove or disprove the following cost-preserving strengthening: for every such instance, every flow $x$ and every cost vector $c \ge 0$, there is an unsplittable flow $\mathcal{P}$ with

  1. $\mathrm{flow}_{\mathcal{P}}(a) \le x(a) + d_{\max}$ for every arc $a \in A$, and
  2. $\sum_{a \in A} c(a)\,\mathrm{flow}_{\mathcal{P}}(a) \le \sum_{a \in A} c(a)\,x(a)$.

Seek a counterexample with demands $15,10,15$, giving each terminal a free route or a paid route costing $30$ for its whole demand. Arrange bottlenecks so additive bounds forbid any two free choices, forcing cost at least $60$, while a feasible fractional flow costs $58$.

Standard, 100-token budget

96 tokens (7.7% of the proof) · N 2/4

Use three demands \(15,10,15\) and give each terminal a free route and a paid route costing \(30\). Aim for fractional cost \(58\), while making every pair of free choices violate an additive load bound, forcing cost at least \(60\). Build a zero-cost backbone \(s\to u\to v\to w\); the crucial asymmetry is that \(t_3\)'s paid route also traverses \(s\to u\).

Standard, 200-token budget

190 tokens (15% of the proof) · N 4/4

Take demands \(15,10,15\). Use the zero-cost spine \(s\to u\to v\to w\), with flow values \(24,14,9\), and zero-cost exits \(v\to t_1,w\to t_2,w\to t_3\). Add paid arcs \(s\to t_1,s\to t_2,u\to t_3\), with respective flow values \(10,6,10\) and costs \(2,3,2\). Determine the exit flows by conservation. Each paid route costs \(30\); the given flow costs \(58\). Since both routes to \(t_3\) use \(s\to u\), the spine bounds \(39,29,24\) respectively rule out free pairs \((t_1,t_2),(t_1,t_3),(t_2,t_3)\). Thus at least two paid routes are forced.
