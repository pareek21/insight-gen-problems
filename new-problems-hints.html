Erdős–Sós (#548): Key step + a line per step · shortest that worked

310 tokens (9.2% of the proof) · K 3/3 · 36K–71K tokens, 11–32 minutes. Every answer follows the hint's plan.

Let \(d\) be the average degree of \(G\), and \(\sigma=(v_1,\dots,v_n)\) a uniformly random ordering of its vertices. For a rooted tree \(T\), call an edge \(v_1v_i\) of \(G\) \(T\)-jumping if \(G\) has a copy of \(T\) with root \(v_1\) inside \(\{v_1,\dots,v_{i-1}\}\), and let \(J_T(\sigma)\) be the number of these edges. Prove \(\mathbb{E}\,J_T\ge d-|T|+1\) by induction on \(|T|\); then apply it to \(T\) minus a leaf, rooted at the leaf's neighbour. If the root has one neighbour, swap \(v_1\) with \(v_i\). If it has degree at least \(2\), split \(T\) at the root into rooted subtrees \(A\) and \(B\): an edge that is \(A\)-jumping in \(\sigma\) and \(B\)-jumping in \((v_1,v_n,v_{n-1},\dots,v_2)\) becomes \(T\)-jumping when the shortest final segment of \(\sigma\) that holds, with \(v_1\), a copy of \(B\) rooted at \(v_1\) is moved, reversed, to just after \(v_1\); this rearrangement is a bijection of orderings.

Feige's conjecture: Key step only: the statement of the Vlassis–Thomas theorem · shortest that worked

168 tokens (9.9% of the proof) · K 3/3 · 97K–175K tokens, 43–46 minutes. Each answer applies the theorem and proves the remaining step itself.

You may cite the Vlassis–Thomas theorem (2026; Gaffke's conjecture): let \(D=(D_0,D_1,\dots,D_n)\) be uniform on the simplex \(\{d\in[0,\infty)^{n+1}:d_0+\dots+d_n=1\}\) and \(K_n(x)=\mathbb{P}\big(\sum_{i=1}^n x_iD_i\le1\big)\) for \(x\in[0,\infty)^n\); if \(Y_1,\dots,Y_n\) are independent and nonnegative with \(\mathbb{E}[Y_i]\le1\), then \(\mathbb{P}(K_n(Y)\le\alpha)\le\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in[0,1]\).

Feige's conjecture: Key step + a line per step

301 tokens (17.7% of the proof) · K 3/3 · 82K–105K tokens, 22–45 minutes.

Shift each \(X_i\) up by \(1-\mathbb{E}[X_i]\): it suffices to show \(\mathbb{P}(Y_1+\dots+Y_n<n+1)\ge(n/(n+1))^n\) for independent nonnegative \(Y_i\) with \(\mathbb{E}[Y_i]=1\). Use the Vlassis–Thomas theorem (2026; Gaffke's conjecture), which you may cite: let \(D=(D_0,D_1,\dots,D_n)\) be uniform on the simplex \(\{d\in[0,\infty)^{n+1}:d_0+\dots+d_n=1\}\) and \(K_n(x)=\mathbb{P}\big(\sum_{i=1}^n x_iD_i\le1\big)\) for \(x\in[0,\infty)^n\); if \(Y_1,\dots,Y_n\) are independent and nonnegative with \(\mathbb{E}[Y_i]\le1\), then \(\mathbb{P}(K_n(Y)\le\alpha)\le\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). Then show that \(x_1+\dots+x_n\ge n+1\) implies \(K_n(x)\le1-(n/(n+1))^n\), noting that the half-space \(\{\sum_{i\ge1}x_id_i\ge1\}\) contains the centroid of the simplex.

Erdős #126: Key step + a line per step · shortest that worked

606 tokens (13.2% of the proof) · K 3/3 · 39K–44K tokens, 10–18 minutes.

Yes; in fact \(|A|\le12\,\omega(A)^2\). Avoid \(S\)-unit equations and compare sums with differences prime by prime. For \(a\ne b\) in \(A\) let \(h_{ab}=\gcd(a,b)\), doubled when \(a/\gcd(a,b)\) and \(b/\gcd(a,b)\) are both odd, and put \(U_{ab}=(a+b)/h_{ab}\), \(D_{ab}=|a-b|/h_{ab}\): integers with \(U_{ab}>D_{ab}\ge1\), and every prime factor of \(U_{ab}\) is in the set \(P\) of \(r=\omega(A)\) primes. For a prime \(p\) and \(T\subseteq A\), call \(\log p\sum v_p(U_{ab})\) and \(\log p\sum v_p(D_{ab})\), over the pairs in \(T\), the \(U\)-mass and \(D\)-mass of \(p\) on \(T\). (1) For every \(p\) and \(T\), the \(U\)-mass of \(p\) is at most its \(D\)-mass plus \(\log p\sum_{e\in M}v_p(U_e)\) for some matching \(M\) on \(T\): group the elements with equal \(v_p\) by the classes \(\pm c\) of their \(p\)-free parts modulo \(p^t\) (modulo \(2^{t+1}\) if \(p=2\)), \(t=1,2,\dots\), and build \(M\) from the deepest level up. (2) Every matching \(M\) on \(T\) has \(\sum_{e\in M}\log U_e\le\frac{6}{|T|}\sum_{a\ne b\in T}\log U_{ab}\) (pairs counted once), by the triangle inequality for \(\log\frac{ab}{\gcd(a,b)^2}\). (3) Summing (1) over \(p\in P\), and using \(\log U_{ab}>\log D_{ab}\), on any \(T\) both the total \(U\)-mass minus the total \(D\)-mass and any one prime's \(D\)-mass minus its \(U\)-mass are at most the weight of \(r\) matchings. Apply the first on \(A\). For each \(p\in P\), split \(A\) into two halves by whether the \(p\)-free part lies in the lower or upper half of the units modulo \(p\) (\(1\) or \(3\) modulo \(4\) if \(p=2\)): all of \(p\)'s \(D\)-mass is inside the halves and \(p\) has no \(U\)-mass there, so apply the second to \(p\) on each half.

The problems

Erdős–Sós (#548)

Erdős and Sós (1963), Erdős problem #548, $100. Solved in September 2026 by GPT-6 Astra in Epoch AI's FrontierMath: Erdős run. True. Reference proof: 3,383 tokens.

All graphs here are finite and simple. A graph $G$ contains a graph $H$ if $G$ has a subgraph isomorphic to $H$ (not necessarily induced).

Let $k \ge 1$ be an integer, and let $G$ be a graph with $n$ vertices and more than $\frac{k-1}{2}\,n$ edges, that is, with average degree greater than $k-1$.

Prove that $G$ contains every tree with $k$ edges.

Feige's conjecture

Feige (2004). Proved on 27 July 2026 by two groups, one with ChatGPT 5.6 Pro, using the Vlassis–Thomas theorem (2026). True. Reference proof: 1,696 tokens.

Let $n \ge 1$, and let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be independent real random variables, each nonnegative with $\mathbb{E}[X_i] \le 1$. They need not be identically distributed, bounded, or discrete. Let $S = X_1 + \dots + X_n$.

Prove that $$\mathbb{P}\big(S < \mathbb{E}[S] + 1\big) \ge \frac{1}{e}.$$

Erdős #126

Erdős and Turán (1934), Erdős problem #126, $250. Solved in August–September 2026 by GPT-6 Astra in Epoch AI's FrontierMath: Erdős runs. True. Reference proof: 4,579 tokens.

For a finite set $A$ of positive integers, let $\omega(A)$ be the number of distinct primes that divide $a + b$ for at least one pair $a, b \in A$ with $a \ne b$; equivalently, $\omega(A)$ is the number of distinct prime factors of $\prod_{a \ne b \in A}(a + b)$. For $n \ge 2$, let

$$f(n) = \min\{\omega(A) : A \text{ a set of } n \text{ positive integers}\},$$

so $f(n)$ is the largest number such that every set of $n$ positive integers has at least $f(n)$ primes dividing its pairwise sums. There is no bound on the size of the elements of $A$.

Determine whether $f(n)/\log n \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$. Prove your answer.
